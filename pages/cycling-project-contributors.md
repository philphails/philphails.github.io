# The People Behind the Cycling Project

The Phil Phails Cycling Project brings together people with different perspectives on cycling, health, performance, and the human experience.

At the center is a real coaching experiment: Phillip Jones working with cycling coach Mark Duroy to become a better cyclist.

But becoming a better cyclist involves much more than riding a bike. It involves training, nutrition, mobility, recovery, psychology, community, and the people who help make the journey possible.

These are the people contributing their knowledge, experience, and perspectives to the project.

---

## Mark Duroy — Velo-Savvy
**Cycling Coach**

Mark is the coach guiding Phillip throughout the cycling project. The series follows their coaching relationship over time, documenting the process of setting goals, building a training plan, putting that plan into practice, evaluating progress, and adapting along the way.

**Areas of contribution:**
- Cycling coaching and training structure
- Performance goals and progress
- Training decisions and adaptations
- The relationship between athlete and coach

[Visit Velo-Savvy](https://velo-savvy.com/)

---

## Never Peak Endurance Specialists
**Endurance Testing, Health & Fueling**

Never Peak brings an endurance and health perspective to the project.

The team includes Dr. Steven Elsbecker, a physician and endurance athlete; Lexi Burdman, an exercise physiologist; and Jade Lee, a registered sports dietitian.

Phillip's involvement includes planned testing at the beginning and end of the project, with the possibility of an additional assessment along the way.

**Areas of contribution:**
- Baseline and follow-up testing
- Exercise physiology and performance metrics
- Nutrition and fueling
- Long-term health and endurance

[Visit Never Peak](https://neverpeakendurance.com/)

## Karl Chiang, PhD
**Psychology, Stress & Health**

Karl Chiang is a board-certified psychologist.

His work includes helping patients cope with life-changing illness, examining stress that may affect quality of life, and addressing health disparities.

He earned his master's and doctorate degrees in counseling psychology from the University of Denver. His training includes a health psychology residency at West Virginia University Health Sciences Center, a psychosocial oncology fellowship at Washington University School of Medicine, and a postdoctoral research fellowship at Brown University.

**Areas of contribution:**
- Stress and quality of life
- Psychological dimensions of health and performance
- Coping with challenges and change
- The relationship between physical activity and well-being
